A day in the notebook

Follow Léa, an electrical engineering student, and her team (Nina, Hugo, Alexis) as they design a hot glue gun. The screen on the right shows what she sees.

08:00

Dashboard

Everything waiting for her in all her Spaces: tickets, requirements, hours to submit, and an “@lea” from Nina.

No email to sort through to know what to do.
08:30

Goldmine

The customer confirms they want to glue one-handed. Léa sets the need to “Customer Confirmed”: the requirements derived from it are justified in one click.

Every requirement answers a real need.
09:00

Diamond

At the bottom of the mine, the functional analysis: the team builds the glue gun's tree (the main function, then the “how” of each branch), attaches the parts, the project risks and one DFMEA line per function, linked to the key test that will prove it.

You design functions, not parts at random.
10:00

Exploration

The multi-month plan recalculates: durations, predecessors, milestones. The Gantt follows, and the Excel round trip still works.

The plan and reality no longer drift apart.
11:00

Agile

Sprint Planning: Backlog → Sprint → Board. Each ticket “implements” a requirement; ECNs go to review.

Today's work is tied to the customer's need.
13:30

Dynamite

The test procedure is written in the wiki, with a live ticket block and Word export in the team's template.

The docs are up to date because they live with the project.
15:00

Testing

She picks the electrical safety and drop tests from the Test Base, generates the Test Plan for the lab, and the PDF result closes the loop.

From requirement to “Verified” test, with no retyping.
16:45

Timesheet

Her hours for the week on the steps of the PDP, copied from one day to the next, submitted to the instructor.

You see where the team's time went, phase by phase.
17:00

Training & Help

A Pugh matrix topic from her training plan, then DEMO to try the new tool risk-free.

You learn where you work.
